Message: age drives both price and loyalty; the price effect shows only at fixed age, and it is small next to the age effect. Builds - Start: grey points, raw trend: lapse falls as price rises. Ask: "So should we raise prices to make customers more loyal?" - Click 1: colour by age group (±2.5 years). - Within a group the tariff barely moves (≈ ±0.6%): price differences are mainly discounts and loadings. - There: higher price → more lapse, slope ≈ +1 pp per +1%. - Groups drift down to the right: older customers pay more AND lapse less. The raw trend is that age effect. - Click 2: the summary. - Click 3: the mechanism. Tariff rises with age, lapse falls with age: every path between price and lapse except θ runs through age (confounding). - θ, the arrow we want, only shows when age is held fixed. Sizes, if asked - Age 25 → 75 at fixed price: −29 pp lapse. - Discounts and loadings at fixed age (SD 2%): typically ±2 pp. The effect we want is the small one. - Raw slope −0.86 = true +1.00 + confounding term Cov(price, g) / Var(price) = −1.86. - The five fitted slopes scatter around 1: sampling noise, plus a slight pull-down from the age range left within each group. Bridge: every model from here on sees age. The question is who separates a small price effect from a large age effect.

Takeaways

Use prediction models to predict, and causal estimators to decide

1

A lapse model is not a what-if

Age drives both price and lapse, and trees go flat beyond observed prices. With price as a feature: 0.82 instead of 1.0

2

Learn from free price variation

Double ML removes what the features predict, then regresses residual on residual: 1.02 (95% CI 0.92–1.11)

3

It changes the decision

Double ML finds the margin optimum at +24%, as in the truth. LightGBM keeps raising prices

What Double ML needs

  1. No hidden drivers of both price and lapse
  2. Free price variation in every segment: discounts, loadings, tests
  3. A response shape, assumed beyond the data

Toy world, 200,000 simulated policies · truth: price effect 1.0, margin optimum +24%

Message: the three things to remember, and the conditions under which Double ML can be trusted. Builds - Click 1, the problem: confounding (age drives price and lapse) and extrapolation (trees flat beyond observed prices). LightGBM with price as a feature: 0.82 at 200k, truth 1.00. - Click 2, the method: remove what the features predict from price and lapse, read the effect from the residuals. θ̂ = 1.02, 95% CI 0.92–1.11. - Click 3, the decision: Double ML's optimum +24% (CI +19% to +30%), index 113, as in the truth. LightGBM: no optimum. - Click 4, what Double ML needs: - All drivers of both price and lapse in the model (violation: agents discount customers who threaten to leave). - Free price variation in every segment: discounts, loadings, A/B tests, tariff changes. Less of it → more data needed (E[V²]). - A response shape beyond the data, testable only inside it (e.g. T² via econml's treatment_featurizer). Close: prediction models to predict; causal estimators, with explicit assumptions, to decide.

Chart - 30 portfolios per size (100 to 200,000 policies). - LightGBM on (age, price), effect = ±2% what-if slope; Double ML with the same LightGBM, 5-fold cross-fitting. - Line = median (Double ML is skewed at small sizes), band = 10th–90th percentile; marker on bottom edge = off scale. Builds - Start: axes and truth. Ask: "Which method gets closer with more data?" - Click 1, LightGBM medians: 0.16 at 2k, 0.63 at 20k, 0.82 at 200k. - From 2k, bias is 2–4× its own spread: precise but wrong. 95% range at 200k (0.73–0.91) excludes the truth. - Click 2, Double ML: - 100–200 policies: nothing works (LightGBM min leaf 50 ≈ constant; Double ML falls to the confounded slope, median −1.12 at 100). - 300–2k: noisy and low (medians 0.75–0.85). - From ≈ 5k: on the truth (median 1.00 at 5k and 200k). SE matches spread (0.049 vs 0.049 at 200k). - 95% CI covers truth in 95% of runs from 500 up (87–100% per size). - Click 3, the formulas ("∼" = shrinks like): - LightGBM: bias = price slope of (f̂ − f), one error. Early stopping keeps f̂ − f small, not flat in price → mutes the weak signal (±2 pp vs 29 pp). - 1,000 fixed rounds → slope 0.999, but hold-out loss worse (0.1901 → 0.1918): only the truth says when to stop. - Double ML: θ̂ − θ = E[(m̂ − m)(ℓ̂ − ℓ)] / E[T̃²] + noise; E[T̃²] ≈ E[V²]. Only the product of the two nuisance errors survives (Neyman orthogonality); g is never fitted, only ℓ and m are. - Textbook bound: ‖m̂ − m‖·(‖ℓ̂ − ℓ‖ + |θ|·‖m̂ − m‖) / E[V²]. - Rates (typical orders, not laws): - Noise n^(−1/2); flexible ML fits e.g. n^(−1/3). - LightGBM: one error ≈ n^(−1/3), falls behind its spread. Double ML: product ≈ n^(−2/3), faster than the noise. - Measured: LightGBM ≈ n^(−0.3); Double ML product term −0.13 (2k), −0.09 (5k), −0.03 (20k), −0.009 (50k), −0.003 (200k) ≈ n^(−0.8). - Formal need: product faster than n^(−1/2), e.g. each error faster than n^(−1/4). - Click 4, what every method needs: the ground rules for any price effect from our own data, LightGBM with price as a feature included. - All drivers of price and lapse in the model (violation: agents discount customers who threaten to leave). - Free price variation in every segment: discounts, loadings, A/B tests, tariff changes. Less of it → more data needed (E[V²]). - A response shape beyond the data, testable only inside it (e.g. T² via econml's treatment_featurizer). LightGBM assumes flat (extrapolation slide), silently. - Difference: Double ML's CI widens when price variation is thin, a visible warning; LightGBM's effect just shrinks into age (the 0.82). Takeaway: prediction models to predict; causal estimators, with explicit assumptions, to decide.

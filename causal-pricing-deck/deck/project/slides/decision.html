Why?

Message: the modelling choice changes the pricing decision, not just a coefficient. LightGBM fails twice: it freezes beyond the data, and its price effect is biased even inside it (next slide). Margin per policy = (1 − lapse(x)) × (premium × (1 + x) − expected claims), claims = 60% of today's premium; index today = 100. Raising prices earns more per retained policy but loses policies, so the true curve has an interior optimum: +24%, index 113. Context: at +24% lapse would be 48% instead of 26.5% today, and this margin ignores expenses and customer lifetime value. Builds. Start: the true curve and its optimum. Pause and ask: "Where would LightGBM set the price?" Click 1, "LightGBM: no optimum": its lapse stops responding from about +12% (extrapolation slide), so every extra percent looks like pure profit. The curve leaves the chart; at +40% it promises 184 where the truth is 109. Click 2, Double ML: lapse after a +x% change = ℓ̂(X) + θ̂·log(1 + x) for every customer, with θ̂ = 1.02 (so +24% adds about 22 pp of lapse, not 24). Optimum +24%, index 113. The band: the margin curves at the two ends of the 95% confidence interval of θ (0.92 to 1.11); it covers the uncertainty of θ only. It is zero at today's price and widens with the distance from the data. Its optimum lies between +19% and +30%; the curve is flat near its peak, so anywhere in that range the true margin is at least 112.7, less than one index point below the best. Click 3, the hook: even inside the data, LightGBM's price effect is too small (0.82 instead of 1.00, extrapolation slide). Why? That is the next slide. Only if asked, "is it just the flat extrapolation?": give LightGBM the same straight-line extrapolation with its own slope of 0.82 per +1%. It then recommends +39% and promises 125; the true margin there is 109 instead of 113. Same extrapolation, still wrong: the slope itself is off. The linearity caveat stays: the optimum lies about six times beyond the observed ±4% price variation, so it rests on the assumed linear response in log price. In this toy that is true by construction; in practice it is an assumption to state openly (see "What Double ML needs" on the closing slide).

Extrapolation

LightGBM on age and price gets the sign right, but freezes beyond the data

Where the data is: price vs age

At any age, observed prices sit close to the tariff; +20% moves every customer beyond them.

Lapse rate after a price change for all customers

Inside the range: right sign, slope too low (0.82 vs 1.0). Beyond it, LightGBM uses older customers’ lapse, then freezes.

Message: including the confounder fixes the sign locally, but the what-if we care about lies outside the data. LightGBM is fitted on (age, price) → lapse and tuned the way we tune for prediction: early stopping on a 20% hold-out (53 boosting rounds here). The what-if: multiply every customer's price by (1 + x), keep age, average the predicted lapse. Builds. Start: where the data is. What matters is not the global price range (older customers do pay +13%) but the price range GIVEN age: observed prices stay within about ±4% of the tariff (±2 standard deviations of the discounts and loadings). Causal-inference name: overlap or positivity. Click 1: the same customers after +20%. No observed 30-year-old pays that much. Click 2: the true lapse curve. Pause and ask: "What will LightGBM predict at +20%?" Click 3: LightGBM. Inside the observed range the sign is right, but the slope is 0.82 pp per +1% instead of 1.00: regularisation shrinks the weak price signal (the closing slide explains why, and how slowly this goes away with more data). Beyond the range it first borrows: at +8% a customer lands at a price only older customers pay, so the model answers with their response (31.7% predicted vs 34.2% true). From about +12%, most customers’ prices exceed anything seen at any age and the prediction freezes: 33% at +20% (truth 45%), 33% at +40% (truth 60%). Why flat: a tree is piecewise constant. Beyond its highest split on price, near the top of the observed price range (about +17% vs the age-25 base), every prediction falls into the same leaf; a sum of trees is flat there too. So LightGBM's lapse does not "go up" at the edge, it stops responding, and its margin then looks like free money (Decision slide). Honest note: every method extrapolates beyond the data only through assumptions. Trees assume "flat"; Double ML with a linear price term assumes "linear in log price". Neither is learned from data out there.

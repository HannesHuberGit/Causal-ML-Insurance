Our setup

In reality customers react differently to price, and the final stage decides how flexibly we capture that

What the toy used

Linear

One price effect, or a simple formula across segments

+ Easy to explain

+ Stable

− One shape for everyone

Most flexible

Boosted final stage

LightGBM learns the effect from the residuals

+ Any shape of effect

+ Any ML model plugs in

− Can chase noise in the residuals

− No uncertainty ranges

Our choice

Causal forest

Trees grown to find where the price effect differs

+ Any shape of effect

+ Honest: resists fitting noise

+ Uncertainty per segment

− Harder to explain than one number

Same first stage in all three; only the last step differs

Message: the toy had one price effect for everyone; real customers differ (young vs old, new vs long-standing, online vs agent). Same first stage; the final stage decides how the effect may vary. Builds: simple → flexible → flexible and reliable - Click 1, Linear (econml LinearDML): what the toy used. - One effect, or a linear formula in a few chosen features. - Explainable, stable, standard CIs. Varies only in the shape we write down. - Click 2, Boosted final stage: - Source: Facure, Causal Inference for the Brave and True (Debiased/Orthogonal ML chapter). - Principled form: R-learner (Nie and Wager, 2021); econml NonParamDML with a LightGBM final model. - Regress Ỹ / T̃ on the features, weighted by T̃². - Most flexible; but where T̃ is small it can chase noise. No CIs out of the box. - Click 3, Causal forest (econml CausalForestDML), our choice: - Splits on effect differences, honest trees, built-in CIs. Why it won: next slide. - Trade-off: a segment-level effect is harder to explain than one number.

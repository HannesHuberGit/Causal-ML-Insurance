Same first stage in all three · the causal forest performed best in our project

Message: real customers react differently to price, so the final stage has to let the effect θ̂(X) vary. Three options; the causal forest is the only one that is flexible, resists noise and gives uncertainty ranges. The first stage is the same in all three. Start: the four criteria. Pause and ask: "What do we need from the final stage?" Click 1, Linear (econml LinearDML). What the toy used: one price effect, or a linear formula in a few chosen features. Stable, with standard confidence intervals, and easy to explain. But the effect can only vary in the shape we write down. Click 2, Boosted final stage: LightGBM learns the price effect from the first-stage residuals, following Causal Inference for the Brave and True (M. Facure, chapter on Debiased/Orthogonal ML). In its principled form this is the R-learner (Nie and Wager, 2021; econml NonParamDML with a LightGBM final model): regress the lapse residual divided by the price residual on the features, weighted by the squared price residual. Any shape of effect. But it uses the same data to choose its splits and to set the values in them, so it can chase noise in the residuals (early stopping helps only partly). No confidence intervals out of the box. Explainable through the usual tools for boosted models (partly). Click 3, Causal forest (econml CausalForestDML), our choice. Any shape, resists noise, confidence ranges per customer or segment. Harder to explain than a single number (partly), the price of flexibility. Click 4, how it does it: - Splits on the effect: a normal tree splits where lapse differs. The causal forest chooses splits that separate customers who react more to price from those who react less, which is the question pricing asks. → "Flexible shape". - Honest trees: each tree uses one half of its sample to choose the splits and the other half to estimate the effect in each leaf. A split that only looks good by chance does not also get to set its own effect → "Resists fitting noise". Because the effect is measured on data the split never saw, the confidence ranges are valid (Wager and Athey, 2018; Athey, Tibshirani and Wager, 2019) → "Uncertainty ranges". Footer: in our project it performed best of the three. Part of the gain is likely the honest trees themselves, not only the flexibility. The assumptions in "What Double ML needs" still hold for all three. Takeaway of the deck: use prediction models to predict, and causal estimators, with their assumptions explicit, to decide.

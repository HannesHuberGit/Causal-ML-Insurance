How Double ML works

Double ML removes what age predicts from price and lapse, then reads the effect

Partially linear model

X: age · g: age effect
V: free price variation

subtract what age predicts, on both sides →

lapse model, price model: what age predicts

1 · Predict lapse from age

Lapse model · first stage

2 · Predict price from age

Slope = price effect · final stage

First stages cross-fitted in 5 folds: each policy is predicted by models that never saw it

Message: Double ML is one equation, estimated in three steps. The grey strip is the whole model; the three columns are how it is fitted. The model (partially linear model). Y = θ·T + g(X) + ε: lapse is a price effect θ·T plus an age effect g(X) of any shape plus noise, E[ε | X, T] = 0. T = m(X) + V: price is what the tariff predicts from age, m(X), plus free price variation V with E[V | X] = 0 (discounts and loadings). The trick. Take the expectation given X: ℓ(X) := E[Y | X] = θ·m(X) + g(X). Subtract it from Y: Y − ℓ(X) = θ·(T − m(X)) + ε (on the slide: ≈, with the noise ε left out). The age effect is gone; θ is the slope of the lapse residual on the price residual, whatever g and m look like (Frisch–Waugh–Lovell with ML in place of linear regressions). ML fits only ℓ and m; g is never fitted directly (the ĝ = ℓ̂ − θ·m̂ in the closing slide's notes is just the age effect these two fits imply). Builds, one per step. Colours: aqua = lapse model, violet = price model, blue = the Double ML price effect. 1. First stage, econml model_y: LightGBM predicts lapse from age, ℓ̂(X); residual Ỹ = Y − ℓ̂(X). 2. First stage, econml model_t: LightGBM predicts price from age, m̂(X), and recovers the tariff; T̃ = T − m̂(X) estimates V. 3. Final stage: least squares of Ỹ on T̃ through the origin (Ỹ ≈ θ̂·T̃ on the slide): θ̂ = Σ T̃·Ỹ / Σ T̃² = 1.02, heteroskedasticity-robust standard error 0.049, 95% CI 0.92 to 1.11. Truth 1.00. The dots are 20 bins of T̃; the regression uses all 200,000 policies. In econml: LinearDML(model_y=..., model_t=..., cv=5).fit(Y, T, X=None, W=age); early stopping inside needs a small wrapper class. Chernozhukov et al. write V for the true price residual; econml calls the estimated residuals T_res and Y_res. Same learner, same tuning as on the extrapolation slide: 0.82 when price is one more feature, 1.02 when LightGBM only handles the age parts. The closing slide shows this is systematic. Expected question: "Would our GLM not do the same?" In this one-factor toy, yes (1.02), because the tariff is linear in age. With many rating factors a GLM needs hand-built interactions and shapes to compete; Double ML lets ML learn them. Cross-fitting (footer): nuisance models trained on 4 folds predict the 5th, rotated. In-sample, a flexible model partly memorises each policy's discount and noise, shrinking the residuals and biasing θ̂. In this toy in-sample gives almost the same answer (1.018); it bites with many factors and flexible learners. Information comes only from V. With a pure tariff (no discounts, loadings, tests or tariff changes) θ is not identified and the confidence interval widens: a warning, not a guarantee (closing slide).

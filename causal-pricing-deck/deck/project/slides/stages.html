Message: from the toy to our setup. Two stages, two jobs, one learner choice each. Strip: the recipe from "How Double ML works", same notation, one step per click. X now = all customer features (rating factors), not just age. Builds - Click 1, lapse model: ℓ̂(X) predicts lapse; Ỹ = Y − ℓ̂(X) = the lapse the features cannot explain. - Click 2, price model: m̂(X) predicts price (the tariff); T̃ = T − m̂(X) = free price variation (discounts, loadings, tests). - Both first stages: pure prediction, judged on hold-out loss, cross-fitted as before. - Click 3, final stage: price effect from the residuals. - Toy: one number, Ỹ ≈ θ̂·T̃. Now θ̂(X) may differ per customer; how flexibly is the next slide. - The causal step: no ground truth to score it → rests on "What Double ML needs". - Click 4, why LightGBM in the first stage. Heading: the same LightGBM that failed with price as a feature; here it only predicts, which it is good at. - Many rating factors: learns shapes and interactions without us specifying them, unlike a GLM. - Categories and gaps: native categorical features (also many levels) and missing values. - Fast: both first-stage models are refitted once per fold. - Only needs to predict well: final-stage bias is a product of first-stage errors (convergence slide). Alternatives, if asked - GLM or Lasso: too rigid for many factors; errors don't shrink, bias stays. - Random forests, neural networks: possible, more tuning. LightGBM = pragmatic default.
